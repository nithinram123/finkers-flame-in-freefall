# Flame in Freefall design

The interface is a NASA research briefing in a single centered column, about 900 px wide. Start with a habitat, show its evidence coverage, then offer a cited question. Keep investigations and individual PSI rows reachable throughout the flow.

Use Inter on a white surface with light neutral groups. Flame orange identifies key actions and measured evidence. Use tint and neutral contrast to group fuels. Keep line icons and 44 px primary controls.

Lead with a short finding. Place measurements, sample counts and source references beneath it. Put experimental detail behind a disclosure or in the source drawer. Format missing values explicitly. Keep initial chamber oxygen separate from burner-stream oxygen.

The chart uses a pressure log scale and counts nearby points. Earth and nominal ISS cabin conditions share a reference at the chart’s resolution. The habitat window uses ±2 oxygen percentage points and ±10% pressure. The coverage meter reports rows within this window. Use NASA PSI accession, source filename and CSV row for original observations. Cabin scenarios and gravity illustrations are labelled separately.

Visual review follows the project brief, with UI UX Pro Max for accessibility and layout, Anthropic Frontend Design for hierarchy, Impeccable’s polish guidance, and Vercel Web Interface Guidelines. Check the rendered desktop and phone flows in Playwright, including keyboard navigation, focus restoration and overflow.
